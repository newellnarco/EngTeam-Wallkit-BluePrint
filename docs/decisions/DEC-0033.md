# The kit ships its failure library and a known-issues intake

## Question

The Patron (2026-09-22): the kit is supposed to ship with a full failure
registry and known-issues intake, built from what MAX3, REEF and MRC already
paid for and genericized. Until now the failure template carried seventeen
seeds and a handful of inherited classes, and the known-issues intake, which
`WALL_STANDARDS` section 4 and `DIAGNOSTICS_LOOP` both depend on, had no
template at all. Where do the corpus and the intake live, and how does a new
repository get them?

## Decision

1. **The library lives in the templates, because `bootstrap` ships them.**
   The genericized failure classes go into the inherited section of
   `templates/FAILURE_PATTERNS.md.template`, marked "not yet observed here"
   and promoted on first occurrence, which is the template's own rule. The
   seventeen seeded classes stay unchanged.
2. **`KNOWN_ISSUES.md.template` becomes the thirteenth template.** It carries
   the intake discipline, the entry format and the generic families, and
   `bootstrap` materializes it as `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`. The template list,
   `TEMPLATE_INTAKE` (section 12), the README runbook and the adopt function
   map all name it.
3. **The kit keeps its own registry and intake at the root.**
   `FAILURE_PATTERNS.md` and `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` hold only what happened in THIS
   repository, and point at the library. They are also the files the kit's
   own scan lane derives ast-grep rules from.
4. **Genericized means public-safe.** The sources are private and this
   repository is public. Entries carry the class, never the incident: no
   hosts, paths, people, PR or issue numbers, or product internals.
   Provenance is one line of deployment codenames and instance counts.

## Why

RECONCILIATION's finding that the design was built blind and then corrected
against a real deployment applies here too: the corpus is that real
deployment's evidence, plus two more. A new repository starts with no memory. The registry exists so it does not
pay twice for a mistake someone already paid for, and three deployments' worth
of paid-for mistakes were sitting in private files a new adopter could not
see. The intake had the opposite problem: two standards depended on a
document that had no template, so every adopter had to invent it.

## What this rules out

A second copy of the library at the kit root (the root holds this repo's own
history only); a corpus under `docs/` that `bootstrap` would vendor but no
template would point at; copying source entries verbatim.

## Revisit if

- The inherited section grows past what a session can read at start-up. Then
  split it into its own vendored document, with the digest staying in the
  template.
- A fourth deployment's corpus arrives. It is genericized the same way, and
  classes seen in two or more deployments are the digest candidates.
