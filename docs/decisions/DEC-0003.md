# Keys are identity; names are labels

## Question

Agents need to be referred to by machines and by people. One identifier, or two?

## Decision

Two, with a strict split.

- **Key** - `<role3>_<6 hex>`, for example `bld_a41f09`. Permanent, never
  reused, and the only thing any event, lease, trace, decision or item stores.
- **Name** - a first name, unique among **live** agents repo-wide, released when
  the agent ends and reusable afterwards. Shown on every human surface, stored
  by nothing downstream.

## Why

The two identifiers answer different questions. The ledger needs something that
can never collide across years of history, because the whole audit property
rests on being able to join records. Humans need something they can say out
loud - "tell Desmond to stop" - and hex is not that.

Making names reusable is only safe *because* nothing downstream depends on them.
That is the load-bearing half of the rule: the moment a name appears in an
event payload, reuse becomes a data corruption bug.

The allocator refuses a name sharing its first two letters with a live agent,
plus a hand-listed confusable set. With one name to identify by, an instruction
meant for Theo landing on Otto is a real failure. Uniqueness is hard;
distinctness degrades gracefully when the pool is tight.

Settled during design and carried through unchanged by the reconciliation
(`docs/RECONCILIATION.md` Part 3), which found nothing in the first live wave
that argued against it - and one thing that extended it: shared mutable state in
the working tree, covered by the operating rules rather than here.

## What this rules out

- Storing display names in events, items, leases or decisions.
- Reusing a key. Ever. A returning agent is a new key with the same name.

## Revisit if

Never, for the storage rule. The naming policy - pool, confusables, release
timing - is tunable without touching it.
