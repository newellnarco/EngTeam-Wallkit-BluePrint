# Budget limits are configuration; budget actuals come from the harness

## Question

Open question 12. Where do the numbers on the ledger tab come from - static
configuration, a live quota call, or manual entry?

## Decision

Split by kind.

- **Limits** are static numbers in `wall.json`. Hand-edited, durable, reviewed.
- **Actuals** are read from the harness. Every subagent completion reports the
  tokens it used, its tool-use count and its duration; the dispatcher writes
  those into the `run_end` event verbatim.

Nothing is estimated for the dominant cost line.

## Why

The actuals were already free and the original design was about to estimate
them. A completion notification carries the real usage, so the only work is
recording it - and a recorded fact beats a modelled one permanently, because
the model has to be maintained and the fact does not.

Limits stay static because they are a policy, not a measurement, and because a
live quota call turns the wall into something that needs network access and
credentials to render. Budget is advisory in this design: meters warn, the human
decides, nothing stops work. A meter that cannot render is worse than one that
renders a hand-entered ceiling.

The value here is the accumulated history rather than any single threshold,
which is why the event schema matters more than the numbers and should be
settled before the first real run.

The split, and the observation that the actuals were already being reported for
free, are `docs/RECONCILIATION.md` Q12.

## What this rules out

- Estimating token usage from prompt length or elapsed time.
- A wall that cannot render without network access.
- A blocking budget gate. Advisory means advisory.

## Revisit if

The harness stops reporting usage per completion, or a hard spending ceiling
becomes a requirement - the second is a policy change the owner makes, and it
lands in the rules, not here.
